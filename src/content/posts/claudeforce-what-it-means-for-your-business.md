If you've seen "Claudeforce" mentioned somewhere in the last few days, here's the two-minute version — no jargon, and no assuming you need to do anything drastic about it this week.

## What it actually is

Salesforce and Anthropic (the company behind the Claude AI models) announced a partnership. In plain terms: the AI running inside Salesforce's Agentforce tools — the thing that drafts follow-up emails, updates records, summarizes a deal — is getting a smarter engine under the hood, and it's more tightly wired into the tools your team already uses. There's also a version that goes the other way: Salesforce data showing up directly inside Claude, so a seller could ask Claude to prep them for a meeting and it pulls the actual account history to do it.

None of this requires you to buy new software. If you're already on Salesforce with Agentforce, this is happening underneath you, on a rolling basis, over the next few months.

## Why it's worth five minutes of attention

The genuinely useful part isn't "AI got better" — every vendor says that every quarter. It's that the access controls stay the same. If a rep doesn't have permission to see a record today, the AI doesn't get to see it either. Nothing about this changes who can see what — it changes how fast the person who *can* see it gets an answer.

## Why it's not worth panicking or rushing, either

A few things are still genuinely unsettled. There's no confirmed answer yet on exactly which version of Claude you'll actually be running. Billing comes as two separate invoices — one from Salesforce, one from Anthropic — so "how much will this cost us" isn't a single simple number yet. And this is a staged rollout: a handful of pilot customers now, a public beta in September, more capability landing through the rest of the year. Early access rarely looks like the finished product.

## Three things worth doing now

![A three-step checklist: ask what's actually included, pilot one workflow, and review outputs before trusting the agent with customers.](/blog/claudeforce-business-checklist.svg)

**Ask what's actually included in your agreement.** Don't assume you have it, and don't assume it's free — check with your account team or your consultant before planning around it.

**Pilot it on one workflow, not your whole sales process.** Pick something small and measurable — meeting prep, or drafting first-touch follow-ups — and give it a few weeks with one team before deciding it's either magic or useless.

**Have a human review outputs before they reach a customer**, at least at first. An AI drafting a follow-up email is a time-saver. An AI sending one unsupervised, based on a summary it got slightly wrong, is a different risk entirely.

## Where a consultant actually earns their fee here

This is exactly the kind of announcement that's easy to either dismiss or overreact to, depending on which headline you read first. The useful role of a technical partner in a moment like this isn't to be excited on your behalf — it's to read the actual announcement, tell you honestly what's real versus what's still a pilot, and help you pick the one workflow worth testing instead of guessing. If your current Salesforce relationship isn't giving you that, that's worth a conversation on its own.
